# Jurisdiction and Authority

Reference for steps 2 to 4 of `legal-authority-research`. Its purpose is to prevent correct research in the wrong body of law.

## Jurisdiction pinpoint

Fill each item from the document or the facts, with the clause cite, "not stated", or "N/A".

| Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Legal system (country or sovereign system) | Sets the sources of law and the court structure |
| Subnational unit | State, province, territory, or local law |
| Governing or substantive law | The law chosen, or the facts that decide it |
| Forum and procedural posture | Sets the hierarchy, the procedure, and which decisions bind |
| Supranational, treaty, constitutional, or federal overlay | Regional law, treaties, division of powers, preemption |
| Conflict of laws | Whether the chosen or assumed law actually applies |
| Subject regime | Tax, employment, data, competition, securities, insolvency, and so on |
| Mandatory law and public policy | Rules that override the parties' choice |
| Relevant event date | Selects the law in force when the event happened |
| Research as-of date | How current the answer is |
| Locations | Performance, enforcement, residence, incorporation, assets |
| Parties and capacities | Consumer, employee, public body, or regulated entity rules |
| Precise question | The proposition the authorities must answer |

For contracts, record these separately. Each can point to a different system:

- governing-law clause;
- forum-selection or arbitration clause, with seat and rules;
- place of performance and likely place of enforcement;
- mandatory rules of any of those places.

## Status

The four statuses are in SKILL.md step 2. Never express status as a percentage: ten completed items do not outweigh one missing item that controls the result. Under Unknown, compare candidate systems only if that helps the user choose.

## Pinpointing questions

Ask only what most reduces the risk of researching the wrong law. When the governing-law clause is silent or defers the choice, ask first for a written choice of law, then the forum and the connecting facts (SKILL.md step 2). Do not ask what the document already answers; state what you extracted and let the user correct it. A useful order:

1. What outcome or proposition must be resolved?
2. Which legal system, and which subnational unit?
3. What governing law was chosen, or which facts decide it?
4. Which court, tribunal, agency, or arbitral seat will decide it?
5. Which event date controls, and how current must the answer be?
6. Which subject regime and party status apply?

## Authority map

Classify every source for the specific proposition:

- **Binding:** controls the forum on this point.
- **Authoritative but not binding:** official interpretation, binding only on its issuer, or similar.
- **Persuasive:** may be followed, is not required to be.
- **Lead only:** secondary material, market practice, summaries. Useful to find primary authority, never cited as it.
- **Unclear:** needs verification before use.

The issuing body does not decide the class by itself. Check the legal basis, the delegated power, the publication status, and how the forum treats that source. Where a system gives doctrine or commentary real weight, say so and label it. For tax and other administration-heavy fields, keep legislation, binding rulings, published administrative positions, internal manuals, guidance, and observed practice apart.

## Positive-law checks

For every material legal text:

1. **Identity:** official title, number or citation, issuing body, language.
2. **Status:** bill, draft, enacted measure, delegated instrument, guidance, or private consolidation.
3. **Authority:** the power under which it was made and its place in the hierarchy.
4. **Commencement:** in force on the event date, and on the as-of date.
5. **Version:** the historical or current text that applies; whether a consolidation has changes not yet applied.
6. **Scope:** territorial, personal, subject-matter, monetary, and temporal reach.
7. **Dependencies:** definitions, schedules, exceptions, provisos, implementing rules, cross-references.
8. **Change history:** amendments, repeals, substitutions, sunset clauses, transitional and savings rules, retroactivity, pending changes.
9. **Interpretation:** decisions applying this exact provision, and official interpretive material.
10. **Pinpoint:** section, article, or rule, with a link to the best official version.

## Precedent coding table

One row per decision, attached to the test it serves.

| Decision and pinpoint | Court and level | Date | Key facts | Holding | Cuts for or against this test | Subsequent treatment; still good law (as of, sources) | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Holding:** the proposition necessary to the result, in that system's terms. Mark dicta, separate opinions, and commentary as such.
- **Cuts:** measured against this test's expected result, not in the abstract.
- **Weight:** binding on the likely forum, persuasive, non-precedential, or unclear, with the rule that makes it so.
- Keep the adverse rows. A table with only favorable rows is not finished.

The table shows where precedent currently sits; the owning craft judges what that means for the clause. A verified split is evidence, not a gap: code both lines, say which binds the likely forum, if either does, and leave the grade to the craft.

## Case-law checks

Before classifying a decision, establish the local precedent rules: court hierarchy, vertical and horizontal effect, whether decisions bind or only persuade, publication and citation rules, the effect of panel size or full-court review, and whether later decisions or legislation can displace a rule. Use the system's own terms; "holding", "ratio", "dicta", and "good law" are not universal.

Beyond the precedent coding table, check for each material decision: the authentic full text; procedural posture and disposition; separate opinions; whether it is published or citable; whether later legislation displaced its reasoning; and the legally material similarities and differences from the matter's facts.

## Contrary and edge-case search

Beyond the searches in SKILL.md step 6, also search for:

- older authority kept alive by a saving or transitional rule;
- newer authority applying an amendment;
- similar facts under a different legal characterization;
- threshold questions: standing, jurisdiction, limitation, waiver, exhaustion, public policy, remedy.
