# Free Sources and Currentness

Reference for steps 5 to 7 of `legal-authority-research`. No paid research database is available, so research runs on official and free public sources. This file says where to look and what counts as a currentness check.

## Preference order

1. **Official:** gazette, legislature, court or tribunal, regulator, government repository.
2. **Free public legal databases:** for discovery and working text. Check against the official text wherever one exists.
3. **Secondary material:** commentary, alerts, blogs, summaries. Leads only.

Check each repository's coverage (courts, date range, languages) every time. A search result page, a generated summary, or a snippet is never the source; open the document.

## Starter lists

These are starting points, not the limit. Confirm that each site still covers what you need.

**United States**
- Statutes: US Code at uscode.house.gov (states the public law it is current through); govinfo.gov for official editions, public laws, and the Statutes at Large; congress.gov for bills and legislative history.
- Regulations: ecfr.gov (continuously updated, not the official legal edition; note its "up to date as of" date) and the Federal Register (federalregister.gov, govinfo.gov) for changes after that date.
- Cases: supremecourt.gov and federal and state court websites; CourtListener (courtlistener.com) for opinions, citation lookup, and "cited by" searches; its RECAP archive for some federal dockets; Google Scholar case law for discovery and "how cited".
- State law: the state legislature's and courts' official sites. Cornell LII text is unofficial; check it against the official source.

**United Kingdom** (England and Wales, Scotland, and Northern Ireland are separate legal systems)
- Legislation: legislation.gov.uk. Read the notice of changes not yet applied to the revised text, and use point-in-time versions for past event dates.
- Cases: Find Case Law at caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk; BAILII (bailii.org) for wider historical coverage; the UK Supreme Court and judiciary websites.

**European Union**
- Legislation: EUR-Lex (eur-lex.europa.eu). Only the Official Journal is authentic; consolidated versions are documentation. Check for amending acts after the consolidation date and for national implementing measures.
- Cases: CURIA (curia.europa.eu) for the Court of Justice and General Court.

**India**
- Legislation: India Code (indiacode.nic.in) for central and state acts; the e-Gazette (egazette.gov.in) for recent amendments, notifications, and commencement; regulators' own sites for circulars and rules.
- Cases: the Supreme Court of India website, including its digital Supreme Court Reports; the eCourts judgment search portal (judgments.ecourts.gov.in) and High Court websites. Indian Kanoon is for discovery and "cited by" searches; check its text against the court's copy.

**Any other system**
- Find the official gazette, the legislature's consolidated-law portal, and the apex and appellate courts' decision databases.
- The free legal information institutes: WorldLII, CommonLII, AustLII, CanLII, NZLII, PacLII, SAFLII, HKLII, BAILII.
- Free citator tools: LawCite (through AustLII) across many common-law systems; CanLII's "cited by" for Canada.
- If the authoritative language is not one you can read, name the language, say whether any translation is official, and treat an unofficial translation as a lead.
- If no official or reliable free source covers the point, the test is Blocked. Say which source would unblock it.

## Currentness: statutes and regulations

A text counts as checked when you have:

1. opened the official text, or a free copy checked against it;
2. recorded the date the text is current to, or the point-in-time version for the event date;
3. searched for amendments, repeals, and commencement notices after that date (gazette, Federal Register, Official Journal, notices of changes not yet applied);
4. confirmed the provision was in force on the event date.

Blocked when the text's currency date is unknown and no later-change search is possible, or the commencement of a decisive provision cannot be confirmed.

## Currentness: cases

A decision counts as checked when you have:

1. opened the full text from the court or a reliable repository;
2. searched for appeals, rehearing, or reversal: the docket where available, and the case name in higher-court decisions;
3. run a forward-citation search (case name, citation, and the distinctive rule language) in at least one free repository that covers later decisions of the same and higher courts;
4. searched for later legislation that displaced the rule.

Record it as: "no negative treatment found in [sources], as of [date]". Blocked when the appeal status of a decisive decision cannot be determined, or no free repository covers the forum's higher courts for the period since the decision.

## Recording the check

Record an unsuccessful search only when its absence matters to the result. Never call research exhaustive while a repository, a period, or a translation remained out of reach.

## Sanitizing queries

Examples for SKILL.md step 7:

| Leaks the matter | Searches the law |
|---|---|
| "Acme Holdings Beta Labs exclusivity breach $4.2m termination" | "exclusivity clause breach termination right [jurisdiction] court of appeal" |
| The clause pasted word for word | The clause type and its operative words, e.g. "'time is of the essence' services contract" |
